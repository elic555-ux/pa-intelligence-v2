# Visual condition and work scope

The draft inference instructions now distinguish the extent of treatment from
the observed condition. Thorough repair can include localized part replacement,
but does not imply wholesale replacement. No replacement is required merely by
choosing that treatment category. Source evidence and rationale remain required.

Multiple views support one component assessment. They must not multiply quantities,
and differing views do not justify averaging away a suspected defect. Scene labels
are not proof that two rooms are the same room. Wall, ceiling and paint work can
overlap and must not be summed as independent jobs without a defined work scope.

Severe stripping or visible corrosion can leave scope unassessable. Request a
targeted professional check without diagnosing hidden structural failure. A polished
image alone does not establish AI editing or completed renovation.

## Corrected development reference

The front-cladding fixture previously used a separate assistant observation as its
reference. The owner's subsequent visual review described very old cladding with
no visible damage established and treatment required, with its scope unresolved.
Reference revision 2 accepts cosmetic/maintenance appearance or no-obvious-damage
in that view, still requires an unassessable unseen roof, and rejects invented rooms,
budgets and incorrect image bytes.

This reference correction does not retroactively turn historical trial outputs into
new model results. Passing the revised case does not demonstrate a model improvement
or general diagnostic accuracy. Historical results retain their original reference.

The existing daily reservation ledger and free-tier-only guards are unchanged.
This code revision does not trigger an inference trial: the explicit diagnostic
trigger file is unchanged. No new measured quantity, unit price or full-property
budget is introduced.
